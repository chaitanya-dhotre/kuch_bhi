The app_lock entry (A1) 
{"v":1, "salt":"…16 bytes b64…", "hash":"…32 bytes b64…", "iter":10000,
 "bio":true, "after":60, "hideNames":true,
 "fails":0, "waitUntil":null}

PIN hash: PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256, 10,000 rounds, 16-byte salt, in compute (≈0.35 s on the Redmi S2, scaled from the measured 3.55 s for 100k). A 6-digit PIN falls to any offline attack whatever the rounds; what protects it is the Keystore around the entry and the wrong-try waits (S5). The hash only keeps the PIN out of storage in the clear.

Not what app lock does: encrypt the database, change what is sent, or touch the E2E keys.

S2

The lock screen (E1–E7, C3)

An overlay from MaterialApp.router(builder:): the app underneath is Offstage (not painted, no taps, no TalkBack, state kept). Keys 64–72 dp circles on surface2; the bottom-left key is the fingerprint key only when it's on and available. Back = the phone's home screen, never a route pop.
S3

Turning it on (B1–B3, A3; decisions 2–4)

Settings→Privacy · App lock · Off→Turn on app lock→Create a PIN→Enter it again→Fingerprint or face? (only if enrolled)→App lock is on.
Weak PINs refused: all six the same, or a straight run up or down (20 PINs). Mismatch on step 2 → back to step 1 with Those PINs don't match. Try again. Back on step 2 or 3 → step 1, nothing saved.

Why no Sign out on the lock screen (decision 8): sign-out keeps this phone's key pair, so signing back in opens every chat. For a Google account, anyone holding the phone with its Google account could do that and walk past the lock. The recovery key is the one secret a phone-holder doesn't have. Wrong reset attempts share the PIN's wait ladder, so the old 6-digit backup PIN can't be guessed here faster than the app PIN.
